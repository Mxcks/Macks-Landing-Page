# Content guidelines for the public Macks Studios website

Source: extracted on 2026-09-23 from the "content resources for developer" package
(`macks-studios-content-strategy.md`, dated 2026-09-22, plus the framework notes it
references). Only guidance that bears on maintaining this website is kept here. Channel
operations, social handles, and personal naming rules were deliberately left out; they
belong to the private strategy repository, not a public-site codebase.

## The one job of this page

Public content exists to turn a stranger into a booked Studio Discovery call. Every
section, sentence, and button is judged against that. Anything that does not serve
pipeline, positioning, or proof is cut.

## Voice

- Plainspoken, direct, warm with an edge.
- No filler, no flattery, no em dashes.
- Concrete over abstract. Say what happens, not how impressive it is.
- Do not make claims the business cannot back. Numbers, counts, and prices appear only
  when Max has confirmed them.
- Nothing goes public without Max's explicit go-ahead. Drafts are shown first.

## Landing-page discipline (from the landing-page anatomy framework)

- One page, one goal, one primary call to action: book a Studio Discovery call. The CTA
  is repeated where a reader has just gained enough confidence to act.
- Headline states the offer plainly. It must not need the rest of the page to explain
  it.
- Lead with the benefit, then show the feature that delivers it.
- Copy length follows offer complexity, not taste. Cut sections that do not earn their
  place.
- Social proof only when it is real and specific. No placeholder testimonials, fake
  metrics, or invented client records.
- Keep navigation minimal; off-goal links bleed conversions.
- Nothing is finished until it has been tested with real readers where a test is cheap.

## Problem-first writing (from the PAS framework)

Used for the "does this sound familiar" section:

1. Problem: name one situation the reader already feels, accurately enough that they
   feel heard.
2. Agitate: make the cost of leaving it alone specific. Stay credible; never let the
   reader wallow, and always point to the way out.
3. Solve: present the Studio process as the way out.

Use gain framing instead of pain framing where the audience is aspirational rather than
problem-aware.

## Constraints that apply to this site

- The public site is for discovery, positioning, and booking. Client operations,
  pricing negotiations, reservations, logins, and proposals live in the separate client
  system.
- The discovery guide is optional, browser-only, and never submits anything. Copy must
  not imply otherwise.
- Do not use internal deliverable names (see `terminology.md`) on the public page
  unless a customer would understand them without explanation.

## Frameworks intentionally not carried over

Content pillars, topic clusters (SEO), hook-value-CTA (short video), content
repurposing, and AIDA were in the source package. They concern posting schedules,
video scripting, and future SEO, not this page. They remain in the private strategy
repository. The package README also notes that those frameworks are reference material
and that "Max's seven business systems" are the real backbone; that set is not defined
in any supplied material (see `macks-product-model.md`, Unresolved).
